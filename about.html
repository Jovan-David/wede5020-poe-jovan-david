About GreenThumb Community Garden

A piece of unused Durban North land became a shared vegetable garden in 2018. Today 240 households grow food on it.

Our history

GreenThumb Community Garden was started in 2018 by eleven neighbours who were given permission to use an empty 1.5 hectare piece of municipal land behind the old sports club. The first season produced four small beds of spinach and beans. Word spread quickly: by the end of 2019 there was a waiting list, a rainwater tank and a Saturday work party that has not missed a week since.

In 2021 the garden registered as a non-profit organisation and opened a seedling nursery. Two years later we added the Berea training hub so that workshops could continue through the rainy season, and in 2025 the youth education plot in Chatsworth welcomed its first school group.

Our mission

To make fresh, affordable food normal in Durban households by sharing land, tools and practical knowledge with anyone who wants to learn. We measure success in harvests, but also in the number of people who can grow a meal from a seed without help.

Our vision

A city where every neighbourhood has a working garden within walking distance, and where food skills are passed from neighbour to neighbour rather than bought.

Who we serve

  • Member households who rent a seasonal plot and keep everything they grow.
  • Local primary schools that use the youth plot for life science lessons.
  • First-time growers who join the beginner workshop or volunteer on Saturdays.
  • Neighbours in need who receive weekly harvest bags from the surplus beds.
How we work

Three rules that keep the garden growing

  • Share the tools. Everything is communal and returned to the shed at the end of the day.
  • Teach one person. Every member who learns a new technique passes it on.
  • Leave the soil better. Compost, mulch and crop rotation are not optional extras.

We follow the water-wise growing techniques recommended for summer rainfall areas, including deep mulching and drip irrigation on the market beds.

Milestones

2018

Eleven neighbours, four beds

The garden starts on unused municipal land with donated seed and one shared hosepipe.

2021

Non-profit status

We register as an NPO, open the seedling nursery and start supplying school gardens.

2025

Harvest market and youth plot

The monthly Umhlanga market funds seedling packs, and the Chatsworth youth plot opens.

Come and see for yourself

Tours run on the first Saturday of every month at 09:00. The path is level and the beds are waist height, so the garden is easy to visit with a pram or a walking frame.
